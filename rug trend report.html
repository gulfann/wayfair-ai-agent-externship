Category Deep Dive

Category Definition

An area rug is a floor‑covering that defines a specific zone within a larger space, adding texture, color, and comfort underfoot. Typical specifications include a pile height ranging from flat‑weave (0 mm) to plush high‑low (up to 20 mm), a construction method such as hand‑tufted, hand‑woven, or machine‑made, and materials like wool, cotton, jute, silk, or synthetic blends. Common use cases span from anchoring seating arrangements in living rooms to providing acoustic dampening in home offices and adding a decorative layer beneath dining tables or bedroom beds.

Sizing Guide

  • Living Room: For a standard sofa set, an 8 × 10 ft rug works well under a 3‑seat sofa and coffee table; larger layouts (sectionals) benefit from a 9 × 12 ft or 10 × 14 ft rug to anchor the entire seating area.
  • Home Office: A 5 × 7 ft rug under a desk and chair provides enough coverage for movement while keeping the space grounded; larger open‑plan offices can use 6 × 9 ft rugs.
  • Minimalist Bedroom: Place a 6 × 9 ft rug centered under the bed to frame the mattress, or opt for a 5 × 7 ft runner if the room is narrow.
  • Dining Area: Choose a rug that extends at least 24‑inches beyond the table on all sides; common sizes are 6 × 9 ft for a 4‑person table and 8 × 10 ft for a 6‑person table.
  • Boho Bedroom: Layer a 5 × 7 ft flat‑weave rug at the foot of the bed and add a smaller 2 × 3 ft runner for added texture.
  • Luxury Living Room / Hotel Lobby: Oversized rugs such as 10 × 14 ft or 12 × 15 ft create a grand, cohesive look and accommodate larger furniture groupings.
